# Task 3.2 Conditional Probability Analysis

## Results

| Metric | Value |
|----------|---------:|
| P(Clear Weather \| Congestion) | 0.2483 |
| P(High Temperature \| Congestion) | 0.2630 |
| P(Congestion ∩ Clear Weather) | 0.0366 |
| P(Congestion) × P(Clear Weather) | 0.0409 |
| Odds Ratio (Clear vs Cloudy) | 0.7352 |

## P(Clear Weather | Congestion)

The conditional probability of clear weather given congestion is 0.2483. This indicates that approximately 24.83% of congestion events occur during clear weather conditions.

## P(High Temperature | Congestion)

The conditional probability of high temperature given congestion is 0.2630. This indicates that approximately 26.30% of congestion events occur when temperatures exceed 292K.

## Independence Test

To test whether congestion and clear weather are independent events, P(Congestion ∩ Clear Weather) was compared with P(Congestion) × P(Clear Weather).

P(Congestion ∩ Clear Weather) = 0.0366

P(Congestion) × P(Clear Weather) = 0.0409

Since these values are not equal, congestion and clear weather cannot be considered completely independent. However, the difference between the values is relatively small, suggesting only a weak relationship.

## Odds Ratio Analysis

The odds ratio comparing congestion in clear weather to congestion in cloudy weather was calculated as 0.7352.

Since the odds ratio is less than 1, congestion is less likely during clear weather than during cloudy weather. This suggests that cloudy conditions are associated with a slightly higher likelihood of traffic congestion.

## Conclusion

The probability analysis suggests that weather conditions have some relationship with traffic congestion, but the relationship is relatively weak. While cloudy weather appears to increase the likelihood of congestion compared with clear weather, traffic patterns are likely influenced more strongly by other factors such as commuting behaviour, time of day, holidays, road conditions and seasonal travel patterns.
